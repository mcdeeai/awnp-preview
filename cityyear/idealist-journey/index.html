Why this version

  • One repeating spine — four questions a member can feel and a Team Leader can run.
  • Outcomes, not themes — every session names what members leave able to do.
  • Fewer stages — collapsed to six so each lands on a day that's actually facilitated.
  • Plain language — "an Applied Wisdom tool," not "Morganism." Keep the skill, lose the jargon.
  • Honest fits — lean on the strong tool-to-stage pairings; don't force the stretched ones.

Two ways to run the same year.

The full hero's-journey arc and the tightened monthly practice each buy something — and each cost something. Here's the honest comparison.

Version A

The full journey

Ten stages · Campbell on the syllabus

Strengths

  • Complete narrative arc — every emotional beat of the year is named and honored.
  • Strong meaning-making; members see a coherent story, not a grind.
  • Generous room for reflection, flexible to different members' experiences.
  • Faithful to the Applied Wisdom source material — substantial and credible.

Costs

  • Dense and theory-heavy; Campbell can feel academic mid-service.
  • Ten stages outrun the ~5 L&D days, so several beats get no session.
  • Themes name topics, not outcomes — harder to know what "done" looks like.
  • "Morganism" branding risks sounding culty; members learn words, not skills.
  • A few tool-to-stage fits are stretched (refusal, resurrection).
Version B

The monthly practice

Four questions · mapped to the calendar

Strengths

  • One repeating spine members can feel and Team Leaders can actually run.
  • Outcome-based — every session says what members leave able to do.
  • Maps cleanly to the real L&D calendar; no orphaned stages.
  • Plain language lowers the barrier and keeps focus on the skill.
  • Consistent and low-prep, so quality stays even across teams.

Costs

  • Loses some of the narrative richness and ceremony of the full arc.
  • Collapsing to six stages drops distinct beats (refusal, reward, resurrection).
  • Repetition can feel like a checklist if facilitated flatly.
  • Campbell is hidden from members — some will want the "why."
  • Leans harder on the Team Leader to keep the four questions fresh.

The likely best of both

Keep the hero's-journey map for facilitators — the richness and meaning live there — but give members the four questions as their monthly habit. Story backstage, practice up front.
